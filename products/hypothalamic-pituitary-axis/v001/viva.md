1. What is the primary function of the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian (HPO) axis?  
The HPO axis regulates female reproduction by controlling ovulation through the release of gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) from the hypothalamus, which stimulates pituitary gonadotropin release [S2].  

2. What hormone is released from GnRH neurons to initiate ovulation?  
Gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) is released from hypothalamic GnRH neurons to initiate ovulation [S1].  

3. How does leptin influence GnRH neuron activity in the hypothalamus?  
Leptin acts indirectly on GnRH neurons via adjacent leptin receptor-expressing neurons such as POMC, NPY/AgRP, and nNOS neurons to modulate GnRH release [S1].  

4. What is the role of hypothalamic inflammation in ovulation disorders?  
Hypothalamic inflammation can disrupt ovulation independently of obesity by impairing hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis function [S1].  

5. What are the three WHO-defined categories of ovulation disorders related to HPO axis dysfunction?  
Group I: hypothalamic failure (hypogonadotropic hypogonadism); Group II: eugonadal disorders with endocrinopathies; Group III: hypergonadotropic hypogonadism due to ovarian dysfunction [S2].  

6. How does the HPA axis respond to stress in females compared to males in preclinical models?  
In preclinical models, females exhibit faster HPA axis activation and higher stress hormone output than males [S3].  

7. What is the primary stress hormone released by the HPA axis?  
Cortisol is the primary glucocorticoid hormone released by the HPA axis in response to stress [S4].  

8. How does chronic stress affect the HPA axis?  
Chronic stress can dysregulate the HPA axis, leading to altered cortisol levels and potential long-term physiological and psychological consequences [S4].  

9. What is the relationship between testosterone and aggression according to the HPG axis?  
Testosterone levels show a positive association with aggression, and androgen receptors in brain circuits modulate aggressive behavior [S4].  

10. How do the HPA and HPG axes interact in regulating behavior?  
The HPA axis mediates stress responses, and glucocorticoids can influence the HPG axis, while androgens like testosterone modulate aggression, indicating cross-axis interactions in behavior regulation [S4].  

11. What is the role of the HPA axis in epilepsy?  
The HPA axis is involved in the stress response and dysfunction is linked to seizure triggers and comorbid psychopathology in epilepsy [S5].  

12. How does early life stress affect the HPA axis in adulthood?  
Early life stress induces persistent HPA axis dysregulation in adulthood, altering glucocorticoid receptor binding and impairing homeostatic balance [S6].  

13. What are the key components of the HPA axis?  
The HPA axis consists of corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH), adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH), and glucocorticoids such as cortisol [S5].  

14. How might obesity disrupt the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis?  
Obesity disrupts the HPO axis by impairing metabolic signaling to GnRH neurons and promoting hypothalamic inflammation, leading to ovulatory disorders [S1].  

15. What clinical feature is most common in obese women related to reproductive function?  
Ovulatory disorders are the most common clinical feature in obese women [S1].
