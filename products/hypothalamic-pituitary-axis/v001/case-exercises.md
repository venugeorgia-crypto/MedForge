**Mini-Case 1: Obesity and Ovulation Disturbance**  
A 32-year-old woman presents with irregular menstrual cycles and infertility. She has a BMI of 34 and reports increased fatigue and weight gain over the past 5 years. Laboratory tests show normal ovarian hormone levels, but no ovulation.  
**Question:** Which hypothalamic mechanism contributes to ovulatory dysfunction in obesity? [S1]  
**Answer:** Leptin acts indirectly on GnRH neurons via adjacent leptin receptor-expressing neurons such as POMC, NPY/AgRP, and nNOS neurons to regulate GnRH release, which is disrupted in obesity [S1].

**Mini-Case 2: Classification of HPO Axis Disorders**  
A 28-year-old woman with amenorrhea and low serum follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and luteinizing hormone (LH) levels is evaluated for infertility.  
**Question:** According to WHO classification, which group of ovulation disorders is characterized by hypogonadotropic hypogonadism? [S2]  
**Answer:** Group I ovulation disorders involve hypothalamic failure, defined as hypogonadotropic hypogonadism [S2].

**Mini-Case 3: Stress and HPA Axis Activation in Women**  
A 25-year-old woman with a history of anxiety and recent trauma reports increased irritability and difficulty concentrating. She has no known medical history.  
**Question:** Compared to men, how does the female HPA axis respond to acute stress according to preclinical models? [S3]  
**Answer:** Preclinical models show that the female HPA axis is activated more rapidly and produces a larger output of stress hormones than in males [S3].

**Mini-Case 4: HPA-HPG Axis Interaction and Aggression**  
A 30-year-old man with a history of intermittent aggression and mood swings is evaluated. Laboratory tests reveal elevated testosterone levels.  
**Question:** Which axis is most closely linked to aggressive behavior through androgen receptor activity in the brain? [S4]  
**Answer:** The hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis, particularly via testosterone and androgen receptors in neural circuits, is closely linked to aggression [S4].

**Mini-Case 5: Early Life Stress and HPA Axis Dysregulation**  
A 24-year-old woman with generalized anxiety disorder reports that her symptoms began in childhood. She has no current medical conditions but experiences frequent panic attacks.  
**Question:** How does early life stress (ELS) affect the adult HPA axis? [S6]  
**Answer:** Early life stress can induce persistent dysregulation of the HPA axis in adulthood, altering glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid receptor binding and disrupting homeostatic balance [S6].
