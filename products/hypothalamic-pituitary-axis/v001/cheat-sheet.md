**Hypothalamic-Pituitary Axis Cheat Sheet**  
*For medical students — concise, cited, evidence-based*

**Definition**  
The hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian (HPO) axis regulates female reproduction via GnRH release from hypothalamic neurons [S2].  
The hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis mediates stress response via CRH → ACTH → cortisol [S3].  
The hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis regulates reproduction via GnRH → LH/FSH → sex hormones [S4].

**Mechanism Chain**  
Metabolic signals (e.g., leptin) modulate GnRH neurons via POMC, NPY/AgRP, and nNOS neurons → GnRH release → pituitary gonadotropin secretion → ovulation [S1].  
Stress activates HPA axis → cortisol → feedback on HPG axis → may suppress GnRH → reduced ovulation [S4].  
Early life stress causes persistent HPA dysregulation → altered glucocorticoid receptor function → lifelong stress vulnerability [S6].

**Must-Know Facts**  
Obesity disrupts HPO axis via hypothalamic inflammation and leptin signaling impairments [S1].  
HPO axis dysfunction is classified by WHO into three groups: hypogonadotropic, eugonadal, and hypergonadotropic [S2].  
Females show greater HPA axis activation during stress than males, though human studies are inconsistent [S3].  
Cortisol from HPA axis can suppress HPG axis activity, impairing fertility [S4].  
HPA axis dysfunction is linked to epilepsy severity and seizure triggers like stress [S5].

**Red-Flag Confusions**  
HPA and HPG axes are distinct but interact; HPA does not directly regulate ovulation [S4].  
Leptin acts indirectly on GnRH via intermediate neurons, not directly [S1].  
“Hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian” ≠ “hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal” — different axes, different functions [S1, S3].  
Early life stress alters HPA axis function long-term, but this does not imply direct causation of adult infertility [S6].

“Stress → cortisol → suppresses GnRH → ovulatory failure” [S4].
