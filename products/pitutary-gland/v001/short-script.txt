Did you know your pituitary gland—just the size of a pea—acts as the body’s master control gland?  

[BODY]  
It sits at the base of the brain and connects to the hypothalamus, regulating hormone release [S2]. The anterior lobe produces key hormones like growth hormone and ACTH, while the posterior lobe stores and releases oxytocin and vasopressin [S2]. Pituitary tumors are common but often asymptomatic—when they do grow, they can cause hormone imbalances, leading to conditions like Cushing’s syndrome or hyperthyroidism [S4]. In rare cases, tumors like prolactinoma can cause galactorrhea or menstrual disruption [S6].  

[RECAP]  
The pituitary gland is central to endocrine function—its hormones regulate growth, metabolism, and other glands [S2].
