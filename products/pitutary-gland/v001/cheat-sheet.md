**Pituitary Gland – Cheat Sheet**  
*Compact, evidence-based, student-friendly*  

---

**Definition**  
The pituitary gland is a pea-sized endocrine gland at the base of the brain, attached to the hypothalamus. It regulates hormone secretion affecting growth, metabolism, and other glands [S2].  

---

**Mechanism Chain**  
Hypothalamus → releases hormones (e.g., TRH, CRH, GnRH, GHRH) → acts on pituitary → anterior lobe secretes hormones (TSH, ACTH, GH, PRL, TSH) → hormones act on target organs (e.g., thyroid, adrenal, gonads) [S2].  
Posterior lobe stores/releases ADH and oxytocin from hypothalamus [S2].  

---

**Must-Know Facts**  
- Anterior lobe: 5 hormones from 5 cell types [S2].  
- Posterior lobe: stores/releases ADH and oxytocin [S2].  
- Pituitary tumors are common but often asymptomatic; most are benign [S4].  
- Hormone overproduction → endocrine diseases (e.g., Cushing’s, hyperthyroidism) [S4].  
- Pituitary tumors may cause headaches, vision loss, nausea, or hormonal imbalances [S4].  
- Local renin-angiotensin system synthesis occurs in extrarenal tissues (e.g., pituitary tumors) [S5].  

---

**Red-Flag Confusions**  
❌ "Pituitary gland = produces all hormones" → No. Only anterior lobe produces hormones; posterior stores hypothalamic ones.  
❌ "Pituitary tumors always cause symptoms" → False. Most are asymptomatic; symptoms arise only when large or hormone-secreting [S4].  
❌ "Chinese herbal medicine cures pituitary diseases" → Not supported. Review shows potential for HPA/HPG axis modulation, not proven therapy [S1].
