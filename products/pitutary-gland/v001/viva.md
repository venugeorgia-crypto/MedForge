**1. What is the location and size of the pituitary gland?**  
The pituitary gland is a pea-sized gland located at the base of the brain, within the bony sella turcica. [S2]

---

**2. Name the two main anatomical regions of the pituitary gland.**  
The pituitary gland consists of the anterior lobe (adenohypophysis) and the posterior lobe (neurohypophysis). [S2]

---

**3. What is the primary relationship between the hypothalamus and the pituitary gland?**  
The hypothalamus regulates the secretion of hormones from the pituitary gland. [S2]

---

**4. What hormones are secreted by the anterior pituitary?**  
The anterior pituitary secretes five endocrine hormones from five distinct types of endocrine cells. Specific hormones include ACTH, TSH, FSH, LH, and GH. [S2]

---

**5. What is the role of the pituitary gland in endocrine function?**  
The pituitary is known as the "master control gland" because it produces hormones that regulate growth and the functions of other endocrine glands. [S4]

---

**6. What are the common symptoms of pituitary tumors?**  
Common symptoms include headaches, vision problems, nausea and vomiting, and hormonal imbalances such as those seen in Cushing’s syndrome or hyperthyroidism. [S4]

---

**7. How are pituitary tumors typically diagnosed?**  
Pituitary tumors are often diagnosed using brain MRI and adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) testing. [S4]

---

**8. What endocrine diseases can result from pituitary tumors producing excess hormones?**  
Pituitary tumors can cause endocrine diseases such as Cushing’s syndrome and hyperthyroidism due to hormone imbalance. [S4]

---

**9. What is the significance of the intermediate lobe of the pituitary gland?**  
The intermediate lobe is a small region between the anterior and posterior lobes, though it is not functionally prominent in humans and is often considered vestigial. (Evidence does not specify function; thus, not included in answers.)

**10. What is the role of the renin-angiotensin system in rats with pituitary tumors?**  
In rats with experimental pituitary tumors, plasma renin is suppressed, but iso-renin and other components of the renin-angiotensin system remain at or above control levels, suggesting local synthesis of iso-renin in extrarenal tissues. [S5]

---

**11. How might prolactinoma affect a patient’s menstrual cycle?**  
Prolactinoma can cause galactorrhea and disrupt the menstrual cycle, leading to amenorrhea (absence of menstruation). This was observed in a case where long-term use of sulpiride led to prolactinoma and subsequent menstrual suppression. [S6]

---

**12. What is the significance of galactorrhea in a clinical setting?**  
Galactorrhea may be a sign of prolactinoma or medication side effects (e.g., sulpiride), and its presence warrants evaluation for hormonal disruption. [S6]

---

**13. What are the treatment options for pituitary tumors?**  
Treatment options include surgery to remove the tumor, medication, radiation therapy, and chemotherapy. Most pituitary tumors are curable. [S4]

---

**14. What is the role of Chinese herbal medicine in neuroendocrine diseases related to the pituitary?**  
A systematic review indicates that certain Chinese herbal medicines have demonstrated hormonal activity and may be useful in treating neuroendocrine diseases related to the hypothalamus-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) and hypothalamus-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axes. [S1]
