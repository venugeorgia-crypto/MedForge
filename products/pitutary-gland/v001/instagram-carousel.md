**Slide 1: Hook**  
🎯 *Did you know? The pituitary gland — a pea-sized structure at the base of your brain — is often called the "master control gland" because it regulates hormones that control growth, metabolism, and the function of other glands.*  
👉 Learn how this tiny gland shapes your body’s hormone balance.  

---

**Slide 2: Anatomy & Location**  
📍 The pituitary gland sits in the **sella turcica** of the sphenoid bone, attached to the base of the brain.  
🔹 It has two main parts:  
- **Anterior lobe (adenohypophysis)** – secretes hormones from five distinct cell types.  
- **Posterior lobe (neurohypophysis)** – stores and releases hormones produced by the hypothalamus.  
🔹 A small intermediate lobe exists between them, though it is often vestigial.  
[S2]  

---

**Slide 3: Hypothalamic Control**  
🧠 The **hypothalamus** regulates pituitary hormone secretion via neural and hormonal signals.  
- Releases releasing and inhibiting hormones (e.g., TRH, CRH, GnRH, GHRH, somatostatin) into the hypothalamic-pituitary portal system.  
- These hormones travel to the anterior pituitary to stimulate or suppress hormone release.  
[S2]  

---

**Slide 4: Key Hormones from Anterior Pituitary**  
The anterior pituitary secretes five major hormones:  
1. **TSH** – stimulates thyroid function  
2. **ACTH** – regulates cortisol (stress response)  
3. **GH** – growth hormone (growth, metabolism)  
4. **Prolactin** – stimulates milk production  
5. **FSH/LH** – regulate reproductive hormones (ovulation, sperm production)  
Each hormone influences specific endocrine axes (e.g., HPA, HPG).  
[S2]  

---

**Slide 5: Pituitary Tumors & Clinical Impact**  
⚠️ Pituitary tumors are common but often asymptomatic.  
- Most tumors are benign and do not cause symptoms.  
- When they grow, they may:  
  - Disrupt hormone balance → cause endocrine diseases (e.g., Cushing’s syndrome, hyperthyroidism)  
  - Compress surrounding structures → cause headaches, vision loss, nausea  
- Symptoms include: headaches, vision problems, galactorrhea, menstrual irregularities.  
[S4]  

---

**Slide 6: Hormonal Disruption & Systemic Effects**  
🔬 A pituitary tumor can alter hormone levels, leading to:  
- **Cushing’s syndrome** (excess cortisol)  
- **Hyperprolactinemia** (e.g., galactorrhea, amenorrhea)  
- **Hypothyroidism** (due to low TSH)  
- **Growth hormone deficiency** → impaired growth or metabolic issues  
These effects arise from overproduction or disruption of normal hormone signaling.  
[S4]  

---

**Slide 7: Research on Pituitary Function**  
🔬 Studies show that pituitary tumors can affect not only classic hormone pathways but also **extrarenal systems** like the renin-angiotensin system.  
- In rat models, pituitary tumors suppress plasma renin but **maintain or elevate iso-renin** and angiotensin components.  
- This suggests **local synthesis** of renin-angiotensin components outside the kidneys.  
[S5]  

---

**Slide 8: Recap**  
✅ The pituitary gland is a small but vital endocrine organ at the base of the brain.  
✅ It works with the hypothalamus to regulate key hormones (TSH, ACTH, GH, Prolactin, FSH/LH).  
✅ Pituitary tumors are common and often asymptomatic, but can cause hormone imbalances and neurological symptoms.  
✅ Research shows disruptions in hormone pathways extend beyond classical endocrinology.  
📚 Evidence from peer-reviewed studies and clinical resources supports these points.
