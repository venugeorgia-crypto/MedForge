[HOOK]  
Acromegaly is a rare endocrine disorder caused by excess growth hormone, often from a pituitary adenoma [S1].  

[BODY]  
It leads to insulin resistance, even in lean patients, increasing diabetes risk—about one-third of patients develop it [S3].  
This condition is linked to higher cardiovascular risks and mortality [S3].  
Diagnosis relies on elevated IGF-1 and lack of GH suppression after glucose testing [S4].  
Pituitary MRI helps identify tumors, and surgery is first-line treatment [S4].  
For larger tumors or post-op failure, medical therapies like somatostatin ligands or pegvisomant are used [S4].  
Older patients show delayed diagnosis and higher comorbidity burdens [S5].  
While cancer links remain debated, long-term GH exposure may increase tumor risk [S6].  

[RECAP]  
Acromegaly causes metabolic and cardiovascular complications, with early diagnosis and treatment critical [S1].
