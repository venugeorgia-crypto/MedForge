1. What is the primary cause of acromegaly?  
Hypersecretion of growth hormone, most commonly due to a pituitary adenoma [S3][S4].

2. What is the key biochemical marker used to confirm acromegaly?  
Elevated serum insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) with lack of growth hormone suppression after glucose administration [S1][S4].

3. How does growth hormone contribute to insulin resistance in acromegaly?  
Growth hormone directly attenuates insulin signaling and stimulates lipolysis, reducing glucose uptake in peripheral tissues [S3].

4. Why is diabetes mellitus common in patients with acromegaly?  
Insulin resistance occurs independently of obesity and is linked to growth hormone exposure, even in lean patients [S3].

5. What is the typical age of onset for acromegaly?  
Most commonly diagnosed during the 3rd to 4th decade of life [S5].

6. Why is acromegaly more frequently diagnosed in the elderly today?  
Due to increased life expectancy, and because aging symptoms overlap with acromegaly manifestations [S5].

7. What is the first-line treatment for acromegaly?  
Transsphenoidal pituitary surgery [S4][S5].

8. What comorbidities are associated with acromegaly?  
Cardiovascular, respiratory, endocrine, metabolic, musculoskeletal, and neoplastic complications [S4].

9. What are the main medical therapies used for acromegaly?  
Somatostatin receptor ligands, cabergoline, and pegvisomant [S4].

10. How does acromegaly affect mortality?  
Increased morbidity and mortality are associated with the disease, especially with prolonged disease duration and high GH levels [S1][S6].

11. What factors are independent predictors of mortality in acromegaly?  
Older age, higher GH levels at last follow-up, higher IGF-1 at diagnosis, malignancy, and radiotherapy [S6].

12. Is there a proven link between acromegaly and cancer development?  
The association remains debated due to limited epidemiological data; long-term growth factor exposure may increase tumor risk, but evidence is inconclusive [S6].

13. What imaging study is recommended in acromegaly patients?  
Pituitary magnetic resonance imaging to identify underlying pituitary adenoma [S4].

14. Why is early diagnosis crucial in acromegaly?  
Delayed diagnosis is common, and early treatment reduces excess mortality and complications [S1][S4].

15. What is the significance of diabetes mellitus in acromegaly patients?  
It is associated with increased cardiovascular morbidity and mortality, and specific management recommendations are currently lacking [S3].
