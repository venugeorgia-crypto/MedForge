## Slide 1: Hook  
Acromegaly is a rare but serious endocrine disorder that silently progresses over years — often going undiagnosed until symptoms become severe.  
It begins with excess growth hormone, leading to abnormal tissue growth and a range of complications.  
Early recognition and treatment are critical to improving outcomes and reducing mortality [S1].

## Slide 2: Cause and Pathophysiology  
Acromegaly results from hypersecretion of growth hormone (GH), most commonly due to a pituitary adenoma [S1].  
This excess GH drives elevated insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) levels, which mediate many of the disease’s effects [S4].  
GH directly impairs insulin signaling and stimulates lipolysis, reducing glucose uptake in peripheral tissues [S3].

## Slide 3: Clinical Manifestations  
Patients present with enlarged hands, feet, and facial features — hallmark signs of acromegaly [S1].  
Other symptoms include joint pain, thickened skin, and organ enlargement [S4].  
Cardiovascular, metabolic, and musculoskeletal complications are common and contribute to morbidity [S4].

## Slide 4: Diagnosis  
Diagnosis is confirmed biochemically: elevated serum IGF-1 and lack of GH suppression after glucose tolerance testing [S4].  
Pituitary magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is recommended to visualize the underlying adenoma [S4].  
These tests are essential for accurate diagnosis and guiding treatment [S1].

## Slide 5: Complications and Comorbidities  
Diabetes mellitus occurs in about one-third of patients with acromegaly, independent of obesity [S3].  
Patients exhibit insulin resistance despite low body fat, making metabolic management complex [S3].  
Increased cardiovascular risk is linked to both acromegaly and its associated diabetes [S3].  
Long-term GH exposure may also increase the risk of benign or malignant tumors [S6].

## Slide 6: Treatment Options  
Transsphenoidal pituitary surgery is first-line therapy for patients with acromegaly [S4].  
However, many patients with macroadenomas do not achieve remission postoperatively [S4].  
Medical therapies such as somatostatin receptor ligands (e.g., octreotide, lanreotide), cabergoline, and pegvisomant are used as primary or adjunctive treatments [S4].  
Somatostatin receptor ligands are generally effective in both initial and postoperative management [S5].

## Slide 7: Prognosis and Risk Factors  
Prognosis is worse in older patients, those with longer disease duration, and those with persistently high GH levels [S5].  
Older age, elevated GH at last follow-up, and high IGF-1 at diagnosis are independent predictors of mortality [S6].  
Mortality improves significantly with disease control and management of comorbidities [S6].

## Slide 8: Recap  
Acromegaly is caused by GH hypersecretion from a pituitary adenoma, leading to elevated IGF-1 and widespread complications [S1].  
Diagnosis relies on biochemical testing and MRI [S4].  
It is associated with diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and increased cancer risk, though evidence remains debated [S3][S6].  
Early diagnosis and treatment are vital to reduce morbidity and mortality [S1][S4].
