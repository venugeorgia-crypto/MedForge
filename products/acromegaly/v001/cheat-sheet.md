**Acromegaly – Cheat Sheet** [S1][S2][S4]  

**Definition**: Acromegaly is a rare endocrine disorder caused by hypersecretion of growth hormone (GH), most commonly due to a pituitary adenoma [S1][S4].  

**Mechanism Chain**:  
GH → stimulates insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) → increased peripheral tissue growth and metabolic disruption → insulin resistance → impaired glucose uptake → diabetes mellitus [S1][S3][S4].  

**Must-Know Facts**:  
- Diagnosis confirmed by elevated serum IGF-1 and lack of GH suppression after glucose tolerance test [S4].  
- Pituitary MRI is advised to identify adenoma [S4].  
- Diabetes mellitus occurs in ~1/3 of patients, independent of obesity, with lean phenotype and reduced adipose tissue [S3].  
- Cardiovascular morbidity and mortality are increased in acromegaly [S3].  
- Elderly patients show rising incidence due to increased life expectancy; diagnosis is delayed due to symptom overlap with aging [S5].  
- Long-term GH exposure may increase risk of benign or malignant tumors; association with cancer remains debated [S6].  

**Red-Flag Confusions**:  
- Acromegaly is not caused by insulin-like growth factor 1 overproduction; GH drives IGF-1 elevation [S1][S4].  
- Insulin resistance in acromegaly occurs without obesity, unlike typical type 2 diabetes [S3].  
- Growth hormone levels at last follow-up and IGF-1 at diagnosis are independent predictors of mortality [S6].  

**Quick Recall Prompts**:  
- “GH → IGF-1 → insulin resistance → diabetes” [S3][S4].  
- “MRI first, then surgery” [S4].  
- “Diabetes in acromegaly: lean, not obese” [S3].  
- “Older patients: more comorbidities, worse prognosis” [S5].  
- “Cancer link? Debated; monitor closely” [S6].  

*Note: Management of diabetes in acromegaly lacks specific guidelines due to limited research [S3].* [S3]
