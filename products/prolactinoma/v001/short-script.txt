Prolactinomas are the most common pituitary adenomas, affecting women most often in their 30s to 40s [S1].  

[BODY]  
Symptoms include irregular periods and galactorrhea in women, and loss of libido in men [S1]. Elevated prolactin suppresses gonadotropin release, leading to infertility [S5]. Dopamine agonists like cabergoline are first-line treatment, normalizing prolactin in up to 85% of patients and shrinking tumors in up to 80% [S2]. Surgery is an option for small tumors or when medication fails [S2]. In postmenopausal women, prolactinomas often stabilize, and treatment may be reduced or stopped with monitoring [S4].
