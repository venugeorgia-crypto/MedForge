1. What is the most common type of pituitary tumor?  
Prolactinomas are the most common secretory pituitary adenoma, constituting about 40% of all pituitary tumors [S1].  

2. What is the typical age and sex distribution of prolactinoma patients?  
Prolactinomas occur most frequently in females between 20 and 40 years of age, with a female predominance during reproductive years [S1].  

3. What are the primary clinical symptoms of prolactinoma in women?  
The most typical symptoms in premenopausal women are oligo/amenorrhea (approximately 90%) and galactorrhea (approximately 80%) [S5].  

4. What is the most common symptom in men with prolactinoma?  
Galactorrhea occurs in about 10% of men with prolactinomas and is virtually pathognomonic of a prolactinoma [S5].  

5. What are the main differential diagnoses for hyperprolactinemia?  
Differential diagnoses include drug intake, hypothyroidism, renal failure, liver cirrhosis, pituitary stalk compression, idiopathic hyperprolactinemia, and other pituitary adenomas [S1].  

6. What is the primary treatment for prolactinoma?  
Dopamine agonists are considered the drugs of choice for the treatment of prolactinoma [S1].  

7. Which dopamine agonist is most effective in normalizing prolactin levels in patients with prolactinoma?  
Cabergoline normalizes prolactin in up to 85% of patients with prolactinoma [S2].  

8. What percentage of patients with prolactinoma experience tumor shrinkage with cabergoline therapy?  
Cabergoline causes tumor shrinkage in up to 80% of patients [S2].  

9. When is transsphenoidal surgery indicated as a first-line treatment for prolactinoma?  
Transsphenoidal surgery is increasingly used as primary therapy for small prolactinomas and is indicated for patients with dopamine agonist intolerance or resistance [S6].  

10. What is the recommended duration and dosage of cabergoline for achieving remission in prolactinoma?  
Treatment with up to 2.0 mg per week of cabergoline for at least 6 months normalizes prolactin and achieves at least 30% adenoma shrinkage [S6].  

11. How does hyperprolactinemia affect gonadal function?  
Hyperprolactinemia inhibits the pulsatile secretion of gonadotropins, leading to suppression of ovarian function in women and testicular function in men [S5].  

12. What is the behavior of prolactinomas after menopause?  
Prolactinomas diagnosed in the reproductive period show stable tumor behavior and prolactin levels after menopause, with treatment often discontinued [S4].  

13. What is the role of multidisciplinary management in prolactinoma care?  
Children and adolescents with prolactinomas should be managed by a pituitary-specific multidisciplinary team due to differences in presentation and etiology [S5].  

14. When is radiotherapy indicated in the management of prolactinoma?  
Radiotherapy is reserved for treatment-refractory prolactinomas [S6].  

15. What is the effect of pregnancy on prolactinoma treatment?  
Dopamine agonists are safe during pregnancy but are typically discontinued at conception with close monitoring for tumor growth [S3].
