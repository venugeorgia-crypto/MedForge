- Prolactinoma  
  - Prolactinomas are benign pituitary adenomas constituting about 40 percent of all pituitary tumors [S1].  
  - They represent approximately half of all pituitary neuroendocrine tumors (PitNETs) [S2].  
  - Most commonly occur in women aged 20–40 years, with a female predominance during reproductive years [S1][S6].  
  - Rarely occur in pediatric populations or after menopause [S3].  
  - In men, prolactinomas are usually larger and more aggressive than in women [S6].  
  - Tumors are characterized by positive immunostaining for prolactin, pituitary-specific positive transcription factor 1, and estrogen receptor α [S6].  

  - Clinical symptoms  
    - In premenopausal women: oligo/amenorrhea (~90%) and galactorrhea (~80%) [S5].  
    - When oligo/amenorrhea is accompanied by galactorrhea, about 75% of women have hyperprolactinemia [S5].  
    - In men: loss of libido and potency [S1].  
    - Galactorrhea is reported in ~10% of men with prolactinomas and is virtually pathognomonic of a prolactinoma [S5].  

  - Differential diagnosis  
    - Must include drug intake, hypothyroidism, renal failure, liver cirrhosis, pituitary stalk compression, idiopathic hyperprolactinemia, and other pituitary adenomas [S1].  

  - Pathogenesis and causes  
    - Hyperprolactinemia results from physiological, pharmacological, or pathological causes such as prolactin-secreting adenomas or stalk-compressing masses [S6].  

  - Treatment  
    - Dopamine agonists (DAs) are the primary treatment for most prolactinomas [S2][S6].  
    - Cabergoline normalizes prolactin in up to 85% of patients and causes tumor shrinkage in up to 80% [S2].  
    - Treatment with up to 2.0 mg per week of cabergoline for at least 6 months normalizes prolactin, achieves at least 30% adenoma shrinkage, and restores gonadal function in most patients [S6].  
    - Primary therapy with DAs has been the mainstay since the 1970s, initially with bromocriptine and now predominantly with cabergoline [S2].  
    - Transsphenoidal surgery is an option when medical therapy is ineffective or poorly tolerated [S1].  
    - Surgery is increasingly used as primary therapy for small prolactinomas due to high remission rates [S6].  
    - Surgery is indicated for patients with dopamine agonist intolerance or resistance [S6].  
    - Radiotherapy is reserved for treatment-refractory prolactinomas [S6].  
    - Multimodality therapy (including high-dose cabergoline, surgery, and stereotactic radiosurgery) is used for aggressive prolactinomas and metastatic PitNETs [S2].  

  - Management in specific populations  
    - In women, dopamine agonists are safe during pregnancy but are typically discontinued at conception with close monitoring for tumor growth [S3].  
    - Breastfeeding is safe postpartum if there was no significant tumor growth during pregnancy [S3].  
    - Postpartum, some women experience normalization of prolactin levels [S3].  
    - After menopause, prolactin levels may decrease, and some patients may consider discontinuing DA agonists with close follow-up [S3].  
    - Prolactinomas diagnosed in the reproductive period show stable tumor behavior and prolactin levels during postmenopause [S4].  
    - Macroprolactinomas diagnosed in postmenopause are included in the literature with limited data [S4].  

  - Impact on health  
    - Prolactinomas are associated with decreased quality of life scores in women [S3].  
    - They may contribute to bone health and cardiovascular risk factors [S3].  

  - Related systems  
    - Endocrine system: hyperprolactinemia inhibits pulsatile secretion of gonadotropins, leading to reduced fertility [S5].  
    - Reproductive system: symptoms include menstrual dysfunction and infertility in women [S1].  
    - Pituitary system: tumors arise from pituitary tissue and may compress the pituitary stalk [S1].  

  - Exam-relevant links  
    - Prolactinomas are the most common functioning pituitary adenoma [S3].  
    - Dopamine agonists are first-line therapy; cabergoline is preferred over bromocriptine due to superior efficacy and fewer side effects [S2][S6].  
    - Tumor shrinkage and restoration of gonadal function are key treatment goals [S6].  
    - Children and adolescents with prolactinomas require multidisciplinary management due to differences in presentation and etiology [S5].
