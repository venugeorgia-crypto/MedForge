**Pituitary Gland – Cheat Sheet**  
*Compact, evidence-based, student-friendly*

---

**Definition**  
Pea-sized endocrine gland at base of brain; regulates growth, metabolism, and hormone balance via hypothalamic control [S2].

---

**Mechanism Chain**  
Hypothalamus → releases hormones (e.g., TRH, CRH, GnRH) → stimulates pituitary secretion → anterior lobe releases hormones (TSH, ACTH, GH, TSH, PRL); posterior lobe stores/releases ADH & oxytocin [S2].

---

**Must-Know Facts**  
- Two lobes: anterior (adenohypophysis) and posterior (neurohypophysis) [S2].  
- Anterior lobe secretes 5 hormones from 5 cell types [S2].  
- Pituitary tumors are common (75–78 per 100,000) [S4].  
- Symptoms: headaches, vision loss, nausea, endocrine dysfunction [S3].  
- Hypersecretion causes: Cushing’s syndrome, acromegaly, hyperprolactinemia [S4].  
- Treatment: surgery (transsphenoidal) first-line for acromegaly/Cushing’s; radiation/medications if needed [S4].  
- Diagnosis: MRI, ACTH testing, physical exam [S4].

---

**Red-Flag Confusions**  
❌ "Pituitary gland produces all hormones" → No; it’s regulated by hypothalamus; only secretes specific hormones [S2].  
❌ "Posterior lobe makes all hormones" → No; it stores and releases ADH/oxytocin from hypothalamus [S2].  
❌ "All pituitary tumors are malignant" → False; most are benign; only 1–2% are cancerous [S3].
