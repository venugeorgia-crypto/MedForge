study-guide.md [no source label]: The pituitary gland is a small, pea-sized endocrine organ located in the sella turcica of the sphenoid bone, at the base of the brain. It acts as the "master control gland," integrating hypothalamic signals to regulate hormone secretion that influences growth, metabolism, reproduction, and stress response. It consists of two main lobes—**anterior (adenohypophysis)** and **posterior (neurohypophysis)**—with the intermediate lobe (now vestigial in humans). Pituitary tumors are common but often asymptomatic; when symptomatic, they may cause endocrine dysfunction or visual field loss.
study-guide.md [no source label]: The pituitary gland functions under **hypothalamic control**. The hypothalamus produces releasing and inhibiting hormones (e.g., TRH, CRH, GHRH, dopamine) that regulate anterior pituitary hormone release. The posterior lobe stores and releases hormones (oxytocin and vasopressin) produced in the hypothalamus. Hormonal imbalances due to tumor growth or dysfunction can lead to conditions like Cushing’s syndrome, acromegaly, or hyperprolactinemia.
